## Spotlight 1 — Nick Ferrarini

**2018 Crusader grad.** High school at Millard North; college ball at Wayne State & Omaha
University. Now an assistant coach at Washburn University, one of the top D2 programs in the
country.

### Q1. Describe your experience playing with the Crusaders. Any significant moments or memories?

My playing experience with the Crusaders was fantastic. I often look back on the Crusaders days as my favorite part of my basketball journey. I think part of what makes AAU so fun is that it's a small look into what college basketball is like. You get recruited to a team, travel across the country together, eat all sorts of meals together, play 3 games in a day and then go hangout and have fun in the hotel and build relationships as teammates. Crusaders were able to provide that for me and left me with lifelong memories and friendships. I ended up committing to Wayne State College because two of my Crusader teammates, Jordan Janssen and Nate Thayer, had already committed there. I think that's a direct reflection of the kind of relationships that were built on those trips and with those teams.

My entire 17U season stands out to me. We had a really good team with pretty much all D1/D2 guys. And this was the first year that a Nebraska AAU program was sponsored by a shoe league and we spent that season playing on the adidas Gauntlet. This was a really cool experience for me and my teammates. This was the opportunity for Nebraska kids to show they belonged on one of the biggest shoe circuit leagues and that we could hang in with some of the best players/teams in the country. On top of that, we dominated stuff here locally. We won the River Cities tournament in July. We won the Sanford Pentagon Series we played in during the Spring. And at the end of the day I was able to have multiple full ride scholarship offers because of the exposure that Crusaders were able to provide for me.

### Q2. What advice could you give student athletes and/or coaches regarding recruiting?

STAY PATIENT, communicate with schools that are showing interest (no matter the level), and continue to work harder than you think you're working. College athletics is changing often and rapidly. There are so many ways for players to be recruited now. I was fortunate when I was playing for Crusaders, and going through the recruiting process, that there was only really two ways to be recruited. Through HS/AAU and JUCO, and that was only 8 years ago! Now, there's the transfer portal, prep schools/academies, waivers for players to get another year of eligibility, and international markets on top of looking at the traditional routes of HS/AAU and JUCO. It can be really easy to get discouraged in the recruiting process but those who don't let that stuff drown them, will reap the benefits.

### Q3. How has grassroots basketball changed? Mostly positive or negative?

In a lot of ways, it's still the same AAU basketball that it has always been and what makes it so fun. Being on the other side of it now, where I'm recruiting as opposed to being recruited, I still see so many of the things I used to see when I was playing. Chaotic games with fun environments and atmospheres. I see this as a positive and think that AAU still has a fundamental part in helping the game of basketball and development for players.

The only negative thought I have for AAU is that it's too oversaturated now with so many different "leagues" or "circuits" that these teams can play on. When I mentioned I was a part of the first shoe sponsored team in Nebraska, there were only three possible leagues to make it in. Nike, Adidas and Under Armour, so there was a level of prestige to it. Now, you not only have those three leagues, but you have Puma, New Balance, NXT Pro, Recruit Look, P32 just to name some! For college coaches, it's made it really hard on budgets for schools that aren't at the D1 level to see kids in their areas. There are so many different teams that play in all these different leagues across the country that it's almost impossible to see a lot of these kids when they play AAU.

## Spotlight 2 — Brooke Delano

**2007 Crusader grad.** Played basketball and volleyball at Bellevue West; turned down multiple
high-D1 basketball offers to play volleyball at Nebraska, where she was Big Ten and All-American.
Now head coach at Roncalli Catholic in Omaha and teaches at Montessori Academy.

### Q1. Describe your experience playing with the Crusaders. Any significant moments or memories?

Playing for the Crusaders gave me so many great memories and opportunities. One of the things I remember most is looking up to the older Crusader girls. As a younger player, they seemed like rock stars!

My favorite memories are the little things — visiting college campus book stores, eating lots of barbeque, The Boo Williams Team, Westside's gym before it had air conditioning, and spending countless hours in the gym with teammates.

### Q2. What advice could you give student athletes and/or coaches regarding recruiting?

Don't rush the process. Choosing a college is about much more than where you'll play—it's deciding who you'll become over the next four to five years. I encourage student athletes to spend as much time as possible with the coaching staff and current players. Those moments will tell you far more about a program's culture than an official visit.

One of the biggest factors in my own recruiting process was finding a coach I could learn from and trust. Whether that's a male or female coach is a personal decision, but it's worth thinking about. Ask yourself whose leadership style, values, and communication will help you become the best version of yourself.

### Q3. How has grassroots basketball changed? Mostly positive or negative?

My daughter is playing select this year, so I'm about to find out firsthand! One change I've noticed is that athletes are specializing in one sport at a much younger age. While I understand why that happens, I'm a big believer in being a multi-sport athlete. Basketball made me a better volleyball player, and volleyball made me a better basketball player. If young athletes have the opportunity to play multiple sports, I encourage them to do it!

### Q4. A day in the life of Brooke

These days, my life is centered around coaching, teaching, family and faith. I'm going into my third season as the head volleyball coach at Roncalli Catholic High School, I also coach my daughter's teams. During the day, I work at The Hills Montessori, where I enjoy helping young people learn and grow!

Vivian keeps me busy, and if we're not in a gym, you can find us roller skating or scrapbooking together.

My brother Billy also lives in Omaha with his four kids, so spending time with them is a big part of my life as well.

Looking back now as a coach, I have an incredible appreciation for the coaches who invested in me—Coach Woodard, Coach Fritsche, Coach Klaus, and Coach Persigehl. They believed in me, challenged me, and never gave up on me. At the time, I didn't fully understand everything they poured into us. Now that I'm coaching myself, I realize how much time, energy, patience, and heart it takes to invest in young people. It's given me an even greater appreciation for the people who shaped me—not just as a player, but as a person.
